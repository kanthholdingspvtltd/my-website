DUSU Election 2026: Results, Key Candidates and What Happened

A factual look at the Delhi University Students' Union election, its results, major candidates and the issues that shaped the campaign.

Published September 2026 • ASTRIX

The Delhi University Students' Union (DUSU) election is one of India's most closely followed student elections. Thousands of students vote for representatives who are expected to raise issues concerning campus life, student welfare and university administration.

The 2026 election was closely watched, with campaigns focusing on issues including student housing, campus infrastructure and safety. Major student organisations including ABVP and NSUI competed alongside independent candidates.

Voting took place on September 18, 2026, followed by counting and declaration of the central-panel results on September 19.

What Is DUSU?

The Delhi University Students' Union represents students across the University of Delhi.

Its central panel consists of four major positions:

  • President
  • Vice-President
  • Secretary
  • Joint Secretary

These representatives are expected to raise student concerns and participate in discussions relating to student welfare and university affairs.

The 2026 Election Results

The final result produced a four-member central panel with three positions going to ABVP-backed candidates and one position going to an independent candidate.

Yash Dabas Wins the Presidency

ABVP's Yash Dabas won the presidential contest with 24,576 votes.

NSUI candidate Vijay Shankar Meena received 22,523 votes, leaving a difference of 2,053 votes between the two candidates.

Presidential result: Yash Dabas — 24,576 votes
Vijay Shankar Meena — 22,523 votes

During the election campaign and after the result, student safety and accommodation were among the issues discussed by the candidates.

Deepanshu Shokeen: The Independent Candidate

One of the most notable results of the 2026 election was the victory of independent candidate Deepanshu Shokeen.

Shokeen received 30,615 votes and defeated ABVP candidate Ishu Maurya, who received 16,910 votes.

Vice-President result:

Deepanshu Shokeen — 30,615
Ishu Maurya — 16,910
Vir Chatrath — 4,313

Shokeen had previously been associated with NSUI and contested the 2026 election independently. His campaign placed significant emphasis on student accommodation and related safety concerns.

ABVP Takes Three of Four Posts

ABVP secured three positions on the central panel:

  • President: Yash Dabas
  • Secretary: Riya Chhawdi
  • Joint Secretary: Lakshya Raj Singh

Riya Chhawdi won the secretary's election with 21,650 votes, while Lakshya Raj Singh won the joint secretary contest with 16,615 votes.

What Happened to NSUI?

NSUI contested all four central-panel positions but did not win any of them.

Its presidential candidate Vijay Shankar Meena finished second with 22,523 votes.

Namrata Chaudhary also finished second in the secretary contest with 14,869 votes.

The result therefore showed that NSUI remained a significant competitor in several individual races, even though it did not secure a central-panel seat.

The Issues Behind the Election

DUSU elections are not only about student organisations. Campaigns also focus on issues that directly affect students.

Student Housing

Accommodation is a major concern for students who move to Delhi from other parts of India. Hostel availability, paying-guest accommodation and safety can have a direct effect on student life.

Campus Infrastructure

Students also raise issues involving classrooms, sanitation, water, internet connectivity and other facilities.

Safety

Student safety became an important subject during the 2026 campaign, particularly in discussions surrounding hostel and PG accommodation.

Student Representation

Another important question is how effectively elected representatives can communicate students' concerns to the university administration.

The Independent Factor

Deepanshu Shokeen's victory demonstrated that an independent candidate could win one of the four central-panel positions.

The joint-secretary contest was also closely fought. Lakshya Raj Singh received 16,615 votes, while Samajwadi Chhatra Sabha candidate Vishesh Yadav received 15,778 votes and NSUI candidate Gopal Choudhary received 15,206.

These results show that individual races can have very different dynamics even within the same election.

What About the Left?

Left student organisations also contested the election.

Their candidates did not win a central-panel position in the 2026 election, but their participation contributed to a multi-candidate contest rather than a simple two-way race.

What Does the Result Mean?

The election produced several notable outcomes.

First, ABVP secured three of the four central-panel positions.

Second, Deepanshu Shokeen won the vice-president's position as an independent candidate.

Third, some contests were relatively close, particularly the presidential and joint-secretary races.

Together, these results provide a picture of a competitive student election involving established organisations and independent candidates.

What Comes Next?

The campaign is over, but the work of the elected representatives has only begun.

Students will be able to judge the new union over the coming academic year by looking at what it does after taking office.

Questions surrounding accommodation, safety, infrastructure and student representation are likely to remain relevant.

The election result provides the starting point. The actions of the elected representatives will determine what happens next.

Conclusion

DUSU Election 2026 produced several different stories within a single election.

ABVP won the president, secretary and joint-secretary positions, while independent candidate Deepanshu Shokeen won the vice-president's post.

The election also brought student housing, safety, infrastructure and representation into focus.

For students, however, the most important part of the election begins after the votes have been counted.

The ballots have been counted. Now the promises have to be tested.
